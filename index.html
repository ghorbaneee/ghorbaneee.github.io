About

Studying flames that only look simple

I am a Ph.D. researcher at the University of British Columbia, working in experimental combustion and sustainable aviation fuel research. My work focuses on understanding how liquid-fuel flames behave under stretch, transport, and thermo-diffusive effects using various flame configurations, optical diagnostics, and flow-field measurements.

I spend much of my time designing and troubleshooting experiments, analyzing complex flame behaviour, and connecting physical observations with combustion modeling tools such as Cantera, MATLAB, and Python. My research combines hands-on experimentation with combustion theory to study flame structure, laminar flame speed, and Markstein behaviour in practical fuel systems.

What interests me most is the physics behind flames that appear simple but are incredibly complex beneath the surface — especially in the context of cleaner propulsion and sustainable aviation energy systems.
